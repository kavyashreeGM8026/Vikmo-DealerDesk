# ML: demand forecast (Stage 1)

`ml/forecast.csv` holds 4-week forecasts (2026-09-28, 10-05, 10-12, 10-19) for the 40 SKUs in `data/lead_times.csv` (160 rows, columns `sku,week_start,forecast_units`). It is generated by `ml/forecast.py` from `data/*.csv`; nothing is entered by hand and the source data is only read.

## Regenerate (Windows PowerShell, from the repo root)

```powershell
python ml\forecast.py
```

Works from any directory (paths are relative to the repo). It runs the backtest, writes `ml\forecast.csv`, and validates the file (schema, 160 rows, 40 SKUs x 4 dates, no duplicates, finite and non-negative). Flags: `--skip-backtest`, `--backtest-only`. Needs only pandas and numpy.

## Method

For SKUs that sell regularly, a pooled log-linear model is fitted on history up to the cutoff:

`log(units) = SKU level + promo effect + trend + Diwali-distance effect`

- **Promotions**: one pooled promo effect (about 2x units in promo weeks in the data). Future flags come from `promo_calendar.csv`.
- **Festive lift**: one effect per week-distance to Diwali (3 weeks after to 5 weeks before), so it follows the festival, not the calendar month. Distances with too little data are left at zero rather than guessed. The 2026 Diwali date (2026-11-08) puts the forecast weeks 5 to 2 weeks before it.
- **Trend**: a single global linear trend.
- **SKU level**: exponentially weighted average (half-life 4 weeks, last 13 weeks) of that SKU's recent residuals after removing the effects above. This adapts to each SKU's current level.
- **New SKUs** (`CHN-1006`, `BRK-1105`, 6 weeks of history): not used to fit the global effects, but their level comes from the same residual average, so they get the pooled promo and festive effects.
- **Rarely sold SKUs** (more than 20% zero weeks: `BRK-1088`, `ELE-1006`, `ELE-1031`, `GRP-1043`): average of the last 13 weeks, constant across the four weeks. They have no promos in the data and too few sales to estimate a festive effect.

A yearly sin/cos term was tried and is off: it improved WAPE by less than 0.001, which is noise.

## Backtest

Rolling origin, 49 weekly cutoffs from 2025-09-22 to 2026-08-24. At each cutoff the model is refitted using only weeks up to that cutoff and predicts the next 4 weeks (using that period's actual promo flags, as the real calendar is known in advance). Training data is filtered at `ml/forecast.py` `backtest()` (`sales["week_start"] <= origin`). The first origin is 2025-09-22 because the same-week-last-year baseline needs 52 weeks of history. 7,464 SKU-week predictions. Measured results:

| Method | WAPE | MAE (units) |
|---|---|---|
| **Selected model** | **0.198** | **7.02** |
| Last value | 0.382 | 13.56 |
| Mean of last 4 weeks | 0.321 | 11.42 |
| Same week last year (falls back to last value if missing) | 0.357 | 12.67 |

Targets inside the Diwali window (836 predictions, 7 origins): model WAPE 0.199 vs best baseline 0.452 (same week last year). Outside it: 0.197 vs 0.289 (recent average). The model beat the best simple baseline in all three cuts.

Hyperparameters (half-life, window, trend, yearly term) were compared on this same backtest, and differences between settings were small (WAPE 0.197 to 0.207), so selection bias is limited but not zero.

## Limitations

- **Rarely sold SKUs are the worst** (per-SKU WAPE 0.8 to 1.2 for `BRK-1088`, `ELE-1006`, `ELE-1031`, `GRP-1043`). Counts of 0 or 1 per week cannot be predicted; the forecast is a flat average and ignores any festive effect.
- **New SKUs** have 6 weeks of history, so the level estimate is noisy. In the backtest they appear only at the last origins (16 predictions, WAPE 0.12), which is too little to trust. There is no launch-ramp model.
- **Only two Diwalis in the data.** The 2025 backtest cutoffs learn the festive profile from 2024 alone, and 2024 starts 4 weeks before Diwali, so the 5-weeks-before effect is estimated from 2025 only. The final forecast uses both years.
- Promo effect is the same for every SKU, and promos are assumed not to interact with the festive lift.
- Shares of the Diwali window are small: only one Diwali (2025) can be backtested out-of-sample.
- Forecasts are medians on the log scale (no mean correction); this scored slightly better on MAE/WAPE than the mean-corrected version.
